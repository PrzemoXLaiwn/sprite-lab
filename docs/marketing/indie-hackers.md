**Title:**

I'm relaunching my abandoned AI side project after rebuilding it from scratch – here's why it failed the first time
```

**Body:**
```
A while back I launched SpriteLab (sprite-lab.com), an AI generator for 2D game assets. It got some visitors and then… nothing. The reason was simple and embarrassing: the generator often ignored the user's prompt. Ask for a knight with a blue shield and you'd get something vaguely knight-shaped. People tried it once and never came back, and honestly I'd have done the same.

I left it alone for a long time. This year I decided to rebuild it from scratch rather than keep patching.

**What I focused on in the rebuild**

1. Doing the core job properly. Prompt → a game-ready sprite as a transparent PNG, in 12 art styles. Pixel art on a real pixel grid with a limited palette, because "AI pixel art" that isn't on a grid is useless in an actual game.
2. One feature that's clearly useful, not ten half-features. For me that's animation: any sprite can be animated (idle, walk, run, attack, jump, fly, turnaround or custom), and the tool suggests motions based on what the sprite is – dog → wag tail, car → drift, dragon → fire breath. Exports sprite sheet, frames and GIF.
3. The boring stuff that saves time: seamless tiles, animated water, icons, projects with auto folder sorting, ZIP export, free upscale.
4. Saying what it does NOT do. No API, no 3D, no 8-direction sets, no autotile tilesets. Being upfront about this is better than refunds.

**Pricing and the relaunch**

- 3 tries without an account, 10 free credits on signup, no card
- Plans from £5/month, commercial use allowed
- Returning users from the old version get +15 credits until 31 Dec 2026 – a small way of saying sorry for the first version
